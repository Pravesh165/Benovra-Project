Benovra is a project that tackles a problem most credit card holders don't even know they have: their cards come with built-in insurance perks, like purchase protection, return protection, and travel-delay coverage, that go unclaimed because people either don't know about them or forget to use them. The result is real money left on the table. Benovra's Card Benefit Activation Engine watches every purchase you make, figures out which protection it qualifies for, and pre-fills an insurance claim for you, so all you have to do is review it, attach the required documents, and hit submit. The system works in five stages: watch, match, pre-fill, document, and approve. First, purchases come in from various sources (manual entry, a live feed, or Stripe webhooks, Stripe is a payment platform). Then a "rule engine" checks each purchase against the protections your card actually includes. If there's a match, the system automatically drafts a claim with all the details already filled in from the transaction. Each type of benefit has its own checklist of required evidence, say, a receipt plus proof of loss for damaged items, and you can't submit until you've uploaded everything. Finally, the claim goes through a structured approval process: small claims auto-approve, larger ones route to a human reviewer, and approved claims get paid out. The system also nudges you when a claim is close to its coverage deadline so you don't lose the window by accident. The audience here is really two groups. Card members get a simple interface showing "Claims to make" and claims already submitted, with a guided wizard for editing drafts, uploading documents, and submitting with one tap. On the other side, reviewers (like bank staff) get a queue of claims to approve or reject, the ability to inspect uploaded evidence, and a dashboard showing metrics such as how much money has been recovered and what percentage of previously unclaimed benefits are now being used. A credit card issuer could use this to increase customer engagement and retention by proving the value of perks people already pay for. Notably, the project is built as a working prototype with honest boundaries: the bank payout and document storage are "mock" implementations behind clean interfaces, so swapping in a real bank connection or cloud storage later wouldn't require rewriting the core logic. It runs entirely on your own machine with a single command, using an in-memory database for demos but ready for a production-grade PostgreSQL database. It also takes security seriously, beyond logins, it enforces strict ownership checks so one member can never see or submit another member's claim.

It is still ongoing not completed yet.
